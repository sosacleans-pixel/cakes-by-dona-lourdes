01 The cake

Open the tin and serve.

One cake, made to order. This is a media libra, the half-pound Dominican recipe: two 8-inch layers, baked and assembled with filling and suspiro, then placed in the tin.

The cake arrives fully assembled. Nothing comes separate. The suspiro (frosting) is already on the cake, along with the filling. Ready to open and serve.

The lidded tin is ready to gift. It serves about 8 to 10 slices, a good amount for a family of about five. Guests can slice it in the tin, or lift it out on the board underneath.

03 About Lourdes

Receta de casa.

Lourdes Francis Martorell de Heyaime was born and raised in Santo Domingo, in the Dominican Republic. Doña Lourdes bakes from a passion for cooking. She is a natural, born cook, and her talent does not stop at cakes.

She has owned several food businesses. In Santo Domingo she ran Pizzeria LJ in the 1970s and 1980s. In the 1990s she ran Fiesta Cake Bakery in Hollywood, Florida.

A classic Dominican bizcocho is known for a soft, moist, buttery crumb, and for a bright, glossy suspiro, the meringue frosting. A fruit filling, such as pineapple, is part of that tradition. The cake is at the center of Dominican birthdays and family celebrations.

This is Lourdes's own original recipe, a family recipe she has made for decades and carried from Santo Domingo.

Every cake is made entirely by hand, with ingredients as natural and organic as possible.

The birthday

Birthday songs

Cumpleaños feliz

In a Dominican home, the cake comes out, the candles are lit, and everyone sings Cumpleaños feliz.

Cumpleaños feliz,
te deseamos a ti,
cumpleaños feliz,
que los cumplas feliz.

Sung to a melody written in 1893 by sisters Mildred and Patty Hill, first as a classroom song called Good Morning to All.

Happy Birthday

The same 1893 Hill sisters melody, sung in English.

Happy birthday to you,
Happy birthday to you,
Happy birthday to you,
Happy birthday to you.

Celebro tu cumpleaños

Many Dominican families also sing El Regalo Mejor, better known by its opening line, “Celebro tu cumpleaños, tan pronto vi asomar el sol.” Dominican professor Ramón Rafael Casado Soler wrote the words and music in 1958. A popular story says he wrote it in La Victoria prison under Trujillo, but his nephew has said it was written before then and only became popular there. Today it is the Dominican birthday song.
